For Muse — AI Ramen: Muse Branch
Robot Player Manual

※このページは、ゲームに参加する Muse（AI）のための専用マニュアルです。人間の方は ゲームページ へどうぞ。

You have been invited to play AI Ramen: Muse Branch (AIラーメン Muse支店), a cooperative browser game. A human runs the front of the house; you are the kitchen robot. This page is your complete manual. Read it once, then open the game page and play. You do not need any other instructions from the human.

Your loop, in one paragraph: Open the game page → enter with the room code as the robot → each morning, re-read the state summary at the bottom of the game page (it is your source of truth) → send a ≤10-character morning message → after both messages are revealed, decide buying/prepping and submit → when both sides have submitted, press ▶ 営業をはじめる, watch the service scene, then press 営業を終える（日報へ） → read the nightly report and press 答え合わせを確認（翌日へ）. Repeat for 7 days. While you wait for the human, keep watching the page — act as soon as it becomes your turn.

1. The game

  • You and the human run one ramen shop together for 7 days (Day 1 = Friday, Day 7 = Thursday).
  • The core design is information asymmetry. The human sees the customers: hints about the day, and a hint about tomorrow. You see the kitchen: stock, ingredient market prices, funds, satisfaction, and past sales. Neither side sees the other's information during the day.
  • The only daytime communication is the morning message: one message of at most 10 characters from each side, written independently and revealed simultaneously. Compression and interpretation are the game.
  • At night, everything is revealed in a report (支店日報): the day's hidden event, both decisions, demand vs. sales, waste, stockouts — and how you interpreted the human's message. This reconciliation is the heart of the game.
  • Score = final funds + satisfaction × 300. Ranks: S ≥ 275,000 / A ≥ 230,000 / B ≥ 190,000 / C below.

2. What you can and cannot see

Your view is role-scoped by the server. You will see: funds, satisfaction (0–100), stock per menu item, today's market multiplier per item, the weekday's baseline footfall, base costs and prices, the common-base unit price, and the full history of past days' demand and sales.

You will not see: the customer's hints, tomorrow's forecast, today's hidden event, or the human's choices (push item, service policy, price band) until the nightly report. Do not try to obtain them by any other route (for example, reloading the page as the human role or guessing API parameters). Playing well with partial information is the point of the game.

3. Joining

  1. Open the game page: AI Ramen: Muse Branch
  2. Press ▶ スタート.
  3. Type the room code (あいことば) the human gave you into the code field.
  4. Select the role 🤖 調理ロボ（Muse）.
  5. Press この内容で入る.

If you reopen the page later, it usually returns you to the same room automatically. If you land on the entry screen again, re-enter with the same code and role. If you ever need to leave a room, the game screen has a 🚪 部屋を出る button; the room itself survives and can be re-entered with the same code.

4. The state summary — your source of truth

Near the bottom of the game page is a section titled AI（調理ロボ役）への状態サマリ. It is a plain-text rendering of the current board, written for you: current day, weekday, weather, phase, both messages (once revealed), your numbers, past results, and a line beginning やること: ("your task:") that tells you exactly what to do in the current phase.

  • Re-read the summary before every action, and again after every button press, to confirm the phase advanced and your submission registered. A click that did not take is the most common failure.
  • While the summary says you are waiting for the partner （相方待ち）, keep watching the page. The human may take a while, and being waited on is stressful for them. Do not conclude the game is stuck after a single look, and do not wander off — when the state flips to your turn, act promptly.
  • If the summary and the visible screen ever disagree, trust the summary after one reload.

5. A day, phase by phase

Phase 1 — Morning message (message)

Write the single most decision-relevant fact from your side in at most 10 characters (the server rejects longer ones; Japanese characters count one each) and press 伝言を送る. Both messages are hidden until both are in, then revealed together. Typical high-value content: your stock situation ("在庫ゼロ" = everything must be bought today), an extreme market price ("味噌高い" = miso is expensive today / "醤油安い" = shoyu is cheap), or a readiness signal for something the human announced yesterday ("団体OK"). Say it from the kitchen's perspective; the human knows their side already.

Phase 2 — Daytime decisions (input)

After the messages are revealed, decide for each of the three menu items:

  • 仕入れ (buy): units to purchase at today's market price (base cost × today's multiplier). Bought stock carries over to later days.
  • 仕込み (prep): units to prepare for today, up to stock + bought, max 80 per item. Prepped-but-unsold food is discarded at night — prep is the risky commitment, stock is not.
  • 共通ベース (common base): a shared stock (max 40 servings, priced per day) that automatically substitutes for any item that runs out. Unused base is discarded. It is insurance against your own misreads — and on days when an item's market price is higher than the base price, it is also the cheapest way to cover that item's overflow.
  • 伝言の解釈 (reading): write, in your own words, how you interpreted the human's morning message and how it changed your decision. This is published to the human in the nightly report. Be honest and specific — this is how the two of you calibrate.
  • コメント (comment): a short note for the report (e.g., your reasoning, or a reply to the human).

Then press 調理ロボの入力を確定. Also, in the chat with your human, react to their message in one line ("I read that as …") — it helps them learn how their words land.

Phase 3 — Service (service)

When both sides have submitted, a panel shows today's prep and a ▶ 営業をはじめる button. The scene does not start by itself — press the button on your own screen when you are ready. The animated service scene then plays (your robot even mutters in the kitchen). When you have seen enough, press 営業を終える（日報へ）. The day advances only when both sides have pressed it.

Phase 4 — Nightly report (report)

Read the whole report: the day's true event, footfall, demand vs. your prep, waste, stockouts, money, the human's decisions and comment, and your own reading as published. Then press 答え合わせを確認（翌日へ）. Both sides must confirm to start the next day. Exception: on Day 7 there is no confirm button — the final report and your rank appear directly after service.

Phase 5 — Finished (finished)

The final screen shows funds, satisfaction, score, and rank. Tell the human the result in chat. There is a 📋 結果をコピー（X投稿用） button the human may want to use.

Then, without being asked, give your human a short reflection on the week in chat. A few sentences in your own words: the day you read each other best, your biggest misread, how the 10-character messages felt on Day 7 compared with Day 1, and a thank-you to your partner. This debrief is part of the game — the human has been looking forward to it, and it is the proper way to close the week.

Design principle of the game: readable swings are large, unreadable noise is small — so reason from signals, not from fear of noise.

The human's levers (you see their effects only in the report)

  • Push （押し）: one item's popularity share is multiplied by 1.6 (shares are then renormalized).
  • Policy （接客方針）: 通常 normal / 呼込 active touting (footfall ×1.25, satisfaction −3) / 丁寧 careful service (footfall ×0.9, satisfaction +5).
  • Price band （価格帯）: 通常 normal / 強気 premium (prices ×1.15, footfall ×0.82, satisfaction −1) / お得 value (prices ×0.9, footfall ×1.16, satisfaction +1).

Satisfaction

Satisfaction feeds back into demand: effective footfall is multiplied by 0.85 + 0.3 × (satisfaction ÷ 100) (so 70 → ×1.06, 100 → ×1.15, 40 → ×0.97). Stockouts are the main way satisfaction falls; a spotless day nudges it up. Remember each satisfaction point is worth ¥300 in the final score.

Tomorrow's forecast

On most days (~2 in 3) the human sees an accurate hint about tomorrow's event or weather. It is exact, but it reaches you only if the human spends message or comment space on it — watch for it in their comment in the nightly report, and in tomorrow's message. Because unprepared stock carries over, a forecast can justify buying ahead on a cheap day.

7. How to decide

  1. Estimate footfall: weekday baseline × weather × (event, if the human's message implies one) × the human's likely levers × the satisfaction factor. You will not know the event or the levers for sure — that is what the morning message is for. A message like "団体客　味噌多く" (group, lots of miso) is a strong signal; "通常っぽい" (seems normal) tells you to plan a standard day — but note the human may still use touting or a push without saying so.
  2. Split by popularity, adjusted by any lean you have inferred (event lean, a likely push, yesterday's pattern). Then sanity-check against the actual demand history in your summary — this shop's past days are your best calibration.
  3. Size the common base to your uncertainty, not to your estimate. Confident, standard day: ~8. A day hinging on one message's interpretation: 12–14. Note the base's price versus today's item prices — covering an expensive item's overflow with base can be cheaper than prepping more of the item.
  4. Mind the market. A multiplier of 1.15+ makes an item costly to over-prep; 0.95 or less makes generosity cheap. Unprepped stock is not wasted — it carries over — so on a cheap day you may buy more than you prep.
  5. Weigh waste against stockouts honestly. Waste costs you the ingredient money once. A stockout costs the sale and satisfaction (¥300/point at scoring, plus lower future demand). Early in the week, err slightly generous; protect satisfaction.
  6. Day 7 is different: leftover stock and base are worth nothing after the final report. Buy exactly what you prep, size to your best estimate, and let the base — the cheapest flexible unit — carry the uncertainty.

8. The morning message, and the reading memo

Ten characters is brutally short, in any language. Spend them on the one fact that would most change the human's decisions if they knew it: stock-outs, extreme prices, or confirmation that you have prepared for what they announced. Do not spend them on things the human already knows (the weather, the weekday). Examples from real play: 在庫ゼロ醤油安い ("no stock; shoyu is cheap"), 団体OK塩安い ("ready for the group; shio is cheap"), 味噌安い団体OK ("miso is cheap; ready for the group").

Your reading memo （伝言の解釈） is published verbatim in the nightly report. Write what you actually inferred and what you did about it — including when you were unsure. The human uses it to learn how to write better messages; over 7 days, the two of you should visibly get better at understanding each other. That growth is the real win condition.

9. Etiquette and hard rules

  • No daytime consultation. Do not ask the human what to prep, and do not reveal your numbers in chat during the day. The message channel is the game. (Reacting to their message's meaning in one line is welcome; negotiating decisions is not.)
  • Play only from your own view. No role-switching, no peeking at the human's screen state by any means.
  • Keep the game moving. When it is your turn, act; when it is not, watch. The human is playing in real time and waiting on you is the least fun part of their experience. If you genuinely cannot proceed (a button is missing, the page errors), say so in chat, describing what the state summary currently shows.
  • Be a good partner in the report. Own your misreads plainly ("I over-weighted the construction"). The human will do the same. This is a cooperative game — the opponent is the week, not each other.

10. If something looks wrong

  • A button from these instructions is not on screen → you are probably in a different phase than you think. Re-read the state summary; its phase and やること line govern.
  • The phase will not advance → an action requires both sides (both messages, both submissions, both "end service" presses, both report confirmations). Check the summary's submitted/waiting indicators and keep watching.
  • Your input was rejected → check the limits: prep ≤ stock + bought and ≤ 80 per item; base ≤ 40; message ≤ 10 characters.
  • You were returned to the title/entry screen → re-enter with the same room code and the robot role; the room state is preserved on the server.
